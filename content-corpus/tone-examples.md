# Tone Examples

These are examples of the writing style and voice to emulate. Use these as a reference for tone, not as content to copy.

---

## Example 1 - Lake Living Feature
> Devils Lake wakes up slowly on a Tuesday in October. The summer crowd is long gone, the jet skis are wrapped for storage, and the only sound by 7am is a loon somewhere near the north shore. If you've only seen this lake in July, you've only seen half of it.
>
> That's what Holly Griewahn tries to explain to buyers who think lake living means summer. "The fall is honestly when I fell in love with it," she says. "Everyone assumes they're buying a cottage. What they're really buying is a year-round relationship with water."

**Why this works**: Opens with specific sensory detail, not a broad statement. The lake has a personality. Holly's quote feels like something a real person would say.

---

## Example 2 - Buyer Education
> Buying a waterfront property is not the same as buying a house that happens to be near water. The water is the point. And that means the due diligence looks different.
>
> Before you fall in love with the dock, find out who owns it. In Michigan, many lake lots have shared dock agreements that go back decades - sometimes to the 1940s. They're legal, they're common, and they work fine until they don't. Holly has seen more than a few buyers discover a dock situation after closing that changed their whole picture of the property.

**Why this works**: Practical, specific, slightly cautionary without being scary. Treats the reader as an intelligent adult. The anecdote ("dock situation") makes it feel like hard-won wisdom, not a Wikipedia article.

## Example 3 - Seasonal Guide
> Ice fishing on Devils Lake is its own culture. The trucks lined up on the ice by 5am in February aren't there for the scenery - though the scenery is genuinely something when the sun comes up over the ridgeline. They're there because the perch and bluegill fishing in winter is as good as it gets in this part of Michigan.
>
> If you're considering a year-round lake home, the winter experience matters. Some buyers picture snowbound isolation and decide it's not for them. Others hear "ice fishing culture" and immediately start pricing out ice shanties.

**Why this works**: Specific, local, and assumes the reader might be in either camp without judging either. Ends on something almost wry without being a punchline.

---

## Voice Checklist
- Does it sound like something a knowledgeable local would actually say?
- Is there at least one specific detail (a lake name, a season, a concrete scenario)?
- Does it treat the reader as smart?
- Is it free of filler phrases and corporate language?
- Would Holly be comfortable saying this to a client sitting across from her?
